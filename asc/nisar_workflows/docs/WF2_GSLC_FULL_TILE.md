**Role in the comparison:** `G_full`. Judged against the RSLC full-tile benchmark (WF1).

## 1. Purpose, scope, and when to use it

WF2 geocodes each RSLC date **independently** onto one pinned UTM lattice (`nisar.workflows.gslc`), then forms
the interferogram, coherence and per-date amplitudes **in map coordinates** by pixel-wise products of the two
GSLCs (`nisar_wf/igram.py`). ISCE3 has **no** ionosphere, troposphere, unwrapping or dense-offset coregistration in
the GSLC path; the split-spectrum ionosphere here is a port (`tools/gslc_ionosphere.py`).

**Use it** for stacks that must share one map lattice across dates, for quick map-domain products, and for
cropping: because geocoding is per-pixel, a GSLC made from a cropped granule is identical to the full-tile GSLC
over the same cells (COMPARISON).

**Know its limits** against WF1: no data-driven coregistration (the two dates are registered by geometry alone),
wrapped-phase agreement with RSLC of 0.887 at 40 m (0.94–0.95 where coherence > 0.5), coherence ~5–6 % lower
multiplicatively (verdicts V2/V3; re-measured in COMPARISON), and an ionosphere whose absolute level rests on a
prior.

Case: pair **20260714 × 20260726**, both frequencies geocoded to **5 m × 5 m**, EPSG:32645, HH.

unwrap → overlay`. Only the first six were run for this case; `watermask`, `unwrap` (full-resolution Track G
unwrapping) and `overlay` were deferred.

### 4.1 Non-obvious behaviour

- **Frequency B is geocoded onto the frequency-A lattice (5 m × 5 m).** Its native ground range is ~38 m, so this
  oversamples B in range roughly 7.6×, costing ~2 h and ~20 GB for the pair (WF2-09). It is required for the
  ionosphere port: on one lattice the A and B interferograms are pixel-aligned and the dispersive solve is
  elementwise, with no decimate/interpolate reconciliation (contrast WF1 4.1). The config comments on `posting.B`
  contradict each other (WF2-31); the value in effect is 5 × 5 m.
- **Flattening uses the geometric slant range.** `gslc.py` never sets `flatten_with_corrected_srange`
  (pybind default false), so ionospheric and tropospheric phase survive into the GSLC × GSLC interferogram — which
  is what makes a split-spectrum port possible. Solid-earth-tide corrections shift the sampling position only.
- **The interferogram is already flattened.** `ref × conj(sec)` of two flattened GSLCs needs no further flattening.
- **Coherence estimator depends on looks.** When `looks_y × looks_x < coherence_window²` (every look setting below
  3×3), `igram.py` computes a 3×3 **sliding** coherence at full resolution (log: "3 x 3 SLIDING window (9
  samples) — the 1 x 1 look box has only 1 sample(s), whose bias floor is 0.886 vs 0.295"); otherwise a boxcar over
  the look box (8×8: N = 64, floor 0.111). Same estimator formula as `tools/slc_coherence.py` (verdicts V3 (a)).
  **Defect:** the sliding window runs per 512-row block without a halo, inflating seam rows by ~+0.05 on 0.4 % of
  rows (WF2-29, OPEN).
- **Freq-B 8×8 coherence overstates independent looks** on the oversampled lattice; the B coherence median (0.609)
  exceeds A's (0.530) for this reason in part (WF2-19).
- **Per-date amplitudes** are √(mean power), each date with its own validity mask; the pair's `.amp` is the
  geometric mean and cannot show per-date backscatter.

### 4.2 The ionosphere port (`tools/gslc_ionosphere.py`)

**Why it works.** GSLC flattening leaves dispersive phase in the interferogram (4.1), and both bands' 8×8
interferograms are pixel-aligned by construction, so ISCE3's own solve applies elementwise. Reused verbatim from
ISCE3: `estimate_iono_main_side` (the 2×2 inversion), `nan_aware_gaussian`, `nan_median_filter`
(`isce3/atmosphere/…`). New: the driver, the unwrap step, cycle-offset resolution, uncertainty and TECU
bookkeeping. `MainSideBandIonosphereEstimation` is **not** instantiated because its `__init__` passes `method` into
the base class's `slant_main` slot (WF2-15).

**Unwrapping is a prerequisite.** The solve is linear in absolute phase:

φ_A = φ_nd + φ_d, φ_B = (f₁/f₀) φ_nd + (f₀/f₁) φ_d, with f₀ = 1.239 GHz, f₁ = 1.2935 GHz.

Each band therefore needs unwrapping (snaphu, 40 m, 7575 × 7850 = 59 Mpx); this is not the deferred full-resolution
Track G unwrap (3805 Mpx).

**The cycle ambiguity dominates the error budget.** Independent snaphu runs fix each band only up to an integer
2π per connected component. One cycle in A adds **+76.17 rad** (+5.59 TECU) to the dispersive term; one in B
**−72.96 rad**. The first run gave **+9.74 TECU** where Track R gave −1.44: 2.0020 cycles of A (WF2-20).

**Resolution, and its limit.** The tool searches integer (m, n) added to (A, B) and minimises
|median(non-dispersive)| — the non-dispersive term is deformation plus residual topography, near zero for a 12-day
pair. Adding one cycle to **both** bands moves the dispersive term by only +3.21 rad (0.235 TECU) and moves the
criterion monotonically, so a plain argmin is unbounded: the first version returned the corner of its search box
(WF2-21). The data determine the **class d = m − n**; the tool takes the minimal-norm member of that class. The
absolute level along the joint-cycle line is therefore a **prior**, uncertain by 0.235 TECU per joint cycle
(WF2-22). Verification (V4) found full-tile and cropped runs agree to 0.006 TECU on identical pixels because both
unwraps' raw inputs differed by exactly +2 cycles in A and 0 in B — the resolver removed exactly that — but the
agreement on absolute level is not guaranteed by the method.

**Noise and filtering.** The inversion amplifies phase noise √(a² + b²) = 16.79×. The raw dispersive layer is
noise-dominated; the filtered layer is the product. The Gaussian σ is specified in **kilometres** (`--sigma-km 10`
→ 250 px at 40 m), because ISCE3's pixel-unit kernel is anisotropic on the radar grid (WF1 4.1). A 15×15 median
conditions the coherence mask (both bands > 0.5 and unwrapped).

**Uncertainty.** σ_φ = √(1−γ²)/(γ√(2N)) per band, combined with the inversion coefficients. The filtered
uncertainty layer was 100 % +inf until a fix defined σ only where both coherences exceed the threshold; the
full-tile layer predates the fix (WF4 log).

**TECU.** `TEC = φ · c · f₀ / (4π K) / 10¹⁶` with K = 40.31 m³ s⁻² (0.0733 TECU per rad at f₀). The comment's
model φ = −(4πK/(c f))·TEC implies the opposite sign; both tracks are converted with the same factor, so relative
comparisons hold, but the **absolute sign is OPEN**, and the earlier "validation against Track R" is circular
(WF2-23).

**Support.** The filtered screen is extrapolated up to ~1000 px beyond valid data by the normalised Gaussian,
so it is finite over no-data corners (WF2-25).

## 5. How to run it

```bash
source /home/sharath/miniforge3/etc/profile.d/conda.sh && conda activate isce3_env
cd /home/sharath/isce3/asc/nisar_workflows
CFG=configs/nepal_glof.yaml

python run_track_g.py -c $CFG --only ingest --frequencies A B --force   # pin BOTH bands
python run_track_g.py -c $CFG --only dem
for F in A B; do                                                         # one band per GSLC run
  python run_track_g.py -c $CFG --only gslc --frequencies $F --dates 20260714 20260726
done
python run_track_g.py -c $CFG --only gridgate --frequencies A B --dates 20260714 20260726
python run_track_g.py -c $CFG --only igram --frequencies A B --igram-freq A --looks 1 1 --dates 20260714 20260726
python run_track_g.py -c $CFG --only igram --frequencies A B --igram-freq A --looks 8 8 --dates 20260714 20260726
python run_track_g.py -c $CFG --only igram --frequencies A B --igram-freq B --looks 8 8 --dates 20260714 20260726

C=/home/sharath/isce3/case_studies/nepal_glof
python -u tools/gslc_ionosphere.py --pair-dir $C/pairs/20260714_20260726/trackG \
   --freq-a-prefix ifg_A_HH_8x8 --freq-b-prefix ifg_B_HH_8x8 --nlooks 64 --coherence-threshold 0.5 \
   --median-filter-size 15 --sigma-km 10 --ntiles 4 4 --nproc 4 --cycle-search 3
```

Notes that matter for automation:
- `--frequencies A B` with `--force` regenerates `stack.json`; the freq-A pin must come out **byte-identical**
  (it did here). Never regenerate `stack.json` while another chain that reads it is running.
- `--dates` restricts every date-enumerating stage through `Config.selected_dates()`; before that fix only
  `gslc.py` honoured it and gridgate demanded an ungenerated date (WF2-02).
- A GSLC file holds the band(s) of the run that made it: `{date}_gslc_freqA.h5`, `…_freqB.h5`, or a combined
  `…_freqAB.h5`. Every consumer resolves the file per (date, band) through `Config.resolve_gslc`.
- Run long stages under tmux with `python -u` and `EXIT=` markers (OPERATIONS_AND_LESSONS.md section 5).

## 10. Known limitations and open questions

- **OPEN** — absolute TEC level (prior along the joint-cycle line) and absolute TEC sign.
- **OPEN** — GSLC coherence is ~5–6 % lower than RSLC multiplicatively; not estimator support, masking or
  inter-date misregistration (GSLC dates registered to 0.02 px); candidate causes (double interpolation,
  deramp/reramp, rotated-spectrum sampling) untested (verdicts V3).
- **OPEN** — the RSLC−GSLC wrapped-phase difference tracks the RSLC dense-offset azimuth residual times the Doppler
  carrier (k = 2π f_dc / 1520 Hz ≈ 3.96 rad/line); which chain carries the residual is not established.
- Freq-B GSLC oversampling cost; full-resolution Track G unwrapping deferred; no water mask for Nepal
  (landlocked; the course recipe's ocean probe does not apply).
- `igram.py` seam defect, nominal snaphu looks, legacy product names, per-stage provenance.

## 12. Automation contract

**Preconditions.** Pinned geogrid for every band to be geocoded; DEM covers the lattice; granules consistent.

**Postconditions.** Each GSLC passes inventory, finiteness and grid gates **before** any cross-date or cross-band
product is formed; interferogram layers carry their looks and coherence estimator (window, N, floor) in metadata;
the ionosphere run records its cycle search table, chosen class and member, σ in km, nlooks, and every output
layer is finite where defined.

**Idempotency and caching.** Key GSLCs by (date, band set, posting, lattice pin, DEM hash); interferograms by (pair,
band, pol, looks, coherence window); ionosphere outputs by (pair, prefixes, nlooks, threshold, median size, σ km,
search width, tool version). A cache hit requires identical keys, not an existing file.

**Failure handling.** A killed GSLC writer leaves a truncated but openable file: detect by inventory, not by
opening. Never regenerate the stack pin under a running consumer.

**Monitoring.** Block counters in the GSLC journal ("running geocode SLC array block i of n"), snaphu tile
progress, RSS during whole-raster statistics, and an `EXIT=` marker per stage.
